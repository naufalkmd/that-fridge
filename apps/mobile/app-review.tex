\section{What ThatFridge does}

ThatFridge is a household food-inventory app. Users track what is in their fridge, freezer
and pantry; the app sends expiry reminders and an AI ``crew'' of four assistants ---
\textbf{Chef}, \textbf{Guardian}, \textbf{Organizer} and \textbf{Shopkeeper} --- suggests
meals from current stock, flags food about to spoil, advises on storage, and builds a
shopping list. A household can share one fridge so every member sees the same live
inventory. Pro unlocks unlimited AI use, receipt/photo bulk-add, and multiple shared
fridges.

\section{Guided walkthrough}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Home tab.} The four crew members, a Kitchen Score, the fridge image
        carousel, and ``crew tip'' cards generated from the seeded inventory (e.g.\ an item
        near expiry, a low-stock item, a recipe suggestion).
  \item \textbf{Inventory tab.} The full item list grouped by storage zone. Tap any item
        for its freshness percentage, expiry date, quantity and notes. Freshness colour:
        green = fresh, amber = due soon, red = overdue.
  \item \textbf{Add (centre ``+'' button).} Four ways to add stock: \emph{Scan barcode}
        (free), \emph{Add manually} (free), \emph{Scan receipt} (Pro), \emph{Photo of
        fridge} (Pro). Barcode and manual entry work on the demo account without a
        subscription.
  \item \textbf{Chat tab.} Ask the crew in plain language, e.g.\ ``what can I cook
        tonight?''. The free tier allows 5 messages per week shared across the crew.
        Responses are produced server-side (see \S\ref{sec:third-party}).
  \item \textbf{Crew tab.} One panel per assistant: Chef (recipes ranked by expiry),
        Guardian (food-safety flags), Organizer (storage guidance and a ``misplaced items''
        sweep), Shopkeeper (shopping list and ``don't rebuy'' hints).
  \item \textbf{Recipes} (from Home or Crew $\rightarrow$ Chef). A recipe book with the
        seeded recipes; open one for ingredients and steps. ``Mark as made'' decrements
        matching items from stock.
  \item \textbf{Sharing} (Profile $\rightarrow$ Fridges, or the ``+'' on the Home fridge
        carousel). Create or rename fridges, invite another user by exact username, and
        approve join requests. Content in a fridge is visible only to approved members.
  \item \textbf{Profile tab.} Subscription status, the upgrade entry point, notification
        settings, ``AI Data \& Memory'' controls, Sign out, and \textbf{Delete account}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reviewing the subscription (Guideline 3.1)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Products.} \emph{ThatFridge Pro Monthly} (\texttt{thatfridge\_pro\_monthly})
        and \emph{ThatFridge Pro Yearly} (\texttt{thatfridge\_pro\_yearly}). Both are
        auto-renewable and both include a 7-day free trial.
  \item \textbf{Purchase path.} StoreKit only, via RevenueCat. There is no external or
        alternative purchase mechanism anywhere in the app.
  \item \textbf{Opening the paywall.} Profile tab $\rightarrow$ \textbf{Upgrade to Pro}, or
        attempt a Pro-only action such as \emph{Scan receipt}.
  \item \textbf{The paywall shows} each plan's price and billing period, the 7-day
        free-trial terms, a \textbf{Restore Purchases} control, and links to the
        \href{https://thatfridge.com/terms/}{Terms of Use} and
        \href{https://thatfridge.com/privacy/}{Privacy Policy}. Auto-renewal and
        cancellation terms are stated on screen and repeated in the App Store description.
  \item \textbf{Sandbox.} Starting the free trial in the review environment grants all Pro
        entitlements immediately; no promo code is required. \textbf{Restore Purchases}
        re-syncs entitlements for the signed-in Apple ID.
  \item \textbf{Cancellation.} Deleting the ThatFridge account does \emph{not} cancel the
        Apple subscription --- that is managed by the user in iOS Settings, per Apple's
        requirements. The app states this at the point of deletion.
\end{itemize}

\section{Answers to likely review questions}

\subsection{Guideline 2.1 --- App completeness}
The demo account is fully seeded, the production API is live, and every AI feature calls a
real service. There are no placeholder screens, debug menus, or ``coming soon'' features in
this build.

\subsection{Guideline 4.2 --- Minimum functionality (not a web wrapper)}
ThatFridge is a native Expo / React Native app. There is \emph{no} web view and no wrapped
website. It makes substantive use of native iOS capabilities:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Camera with its own permission flow (barcode scanning, receipt capture, fridge photos).
  \item Photo library access (receipt and fridge photos, recipe attachments).
  \item On-device speech recognition (Apple's Speech framework, forced on-device --- audio
        never leaves the phone) for voice dictation in chat.
  \item Local notifications scheduled and delivered on-device for expiry and low-stock
        reminders.
  \item Remote push notifications (APNs via Expo) for shared-fridge activity.
  \item Haptics, gesture-driven drag-and-drop, a native navigation stack, and native modal
        sheets.
  \item Sign in with Apple through the native framework.
  \item StoreKit in-app purchases with a native paywall.
\end{itemize}
It also provides functionality with no meaningful web equivalent: AI inventory extraction
from photographed receipts and fridge contents, multi-user shared-fridge sync, and
personalised meal suggestions from the household's live stock.

\subsection{Guideline 4.8 --- Login services}
The app offers \textbf{Sign in with Apple} alongside email/password. Sign in with Apple
meets the requirements: data is limited to name and email, it is not used for advertising,
and there is no associated tracking.

\subsection{Guideline 5.1.1 --- Data collection and storage}
\textbf{Collected:} account name, username and email; user-created content (items, notes,
chat messages); photos the user chooses to attach; subscription status (via RevenueCat); an
Expo push token; and product-interaction history used to compute the Kitchen Score.
\textbf{Not collected:} location, contacts, health data, and card/financial data (Apple
handles payment). No advertising identifiers, no third-party analytics, no tracking SDKs.
The App Privacy section and \href{https://thatfridge.com/privacy/}{thatfridge.com/privacy}
carry the full detail.

\subsection{Guideline 5.1.1(v) --- Account deletion}
Profile tab $\rightarrow$ \textbf{Delete account}, behind a two-step confirmation. This
permanently deletes the account and all associated data on the server; owned fridges,
items, shopping list, recipes and chat history are removed by database cascade. There is no
soft-delete and no hidden retention period --- deletion is immediate and irreversible.

\subsection{Guidelines 1.2 and 5.1.2 --- User-generated content and safety}
The only user-generated content is text (fridge names, sticky notes, recipe notes) and chat
messages. \textbf{None of it is public.} Content in a shared fridge is visible only to
members that the fridge owner has explicitly invited and approved; there is no public feed,
no content discovery, and no browsing of other users beyond an exact-username search.
Safety controls:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Block:} a user can block another user from that user's profile. Blocking is
        bidirectional and prevents search, invitations and join requests in both
        directions.
  \item \textbf{Report:} a \emph{Report} action on a profile sends a report to our support
        team.
  \item \textbf{Contact:} our support address is published in the app and at
        \href{https://thatfridge.com/support}{thatfridge.com/support}.
\end{itemize}
Because sharing is confined to a small, self-selected group, there is no public surface
that would require automated content filtering.

\subsection{Guideline 2.3 --- Accurate metadata}
The description, screenshots and keywords describe only features present in this build. The
app contains no advertising. ``AI crew'' refers to the four in-app assistants; every AI
response is scoped to food and the kitchen.

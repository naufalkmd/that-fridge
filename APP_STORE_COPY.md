The title and subtitle are deliberately broad. ThatFridge is now an inventory, meal planner, recipe book, AI chef and
kitchen insights, so "expiry" alone under-sells it. Expiry stays searchable: it (and "tracker", "waste", "leftovers") sits in the
keyword field, and the promo text and screenshots still lead with it.

Why these words:
- Title + subtitle already carry "kitchen", "assistant", "meal", "plan", "pantry" and "recipes", so those are **not** repeated in the keyword field.
- With the alternative subtitle, "pantry" and "recipes" move into the keyword field and "waste" comes out (the subtitle has it), so the second string is different, not just longer.
- "AI" and "chef" are high-interest words; they live in the keyword field with the recommended subtitle, and in the subtitle itself with the alternative.
- No spaces after commas, and no plurals of a word you already have (Apple matches both).

## Other things that move ranking and installs

- **Localise.** Each locale you add gets its own title, subtitle and keyword slots. Malaysia is the launch market: add **Malay (ms)**, and English for **UK, Australia, Singapore**.
- **Ratings.** They count towards ranking; keep the in-app prompt at good moments (marking a recipe made, finishing the checklist).
- **Category.** Keep Food & Drink as primary. A secondary of Health & Fitness (calories, nutrition) or Lifestyle is worth testing.
- **In-app events** are shown in search and on the product page: a "Sunday meal-plan" event is cheap to set up.
- **One change at a time**, then wait a week or two before judging it, or you can't tell what worked.
